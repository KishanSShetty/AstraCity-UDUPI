## Abstract

AstraCity is a digital twin system for the management of municipal solid waste (MSW), designed for the ward level, which combines geospatial observation, waste flow estimation, route optimization and environment impacts analysis into an integrated decision support system. The twin mirrors are applied on Udupi City, Udupi, and the geo-referenced buildings, road classified segments, and six Dry Waste Collection Centres (DWCCs) serve as the dataset. Maximum likelihood classification (MLC) applied to sentinel-2 imagery identifies 15.3% of open land and the classification is checked against a risk of illegal dump; this is done by applying a DBSCAN clustering algorithm. 54 scenarios are used for modelling across waste generation. A Capacitated Vehicle Routing Problem with Time Windows (CVRPTW) with solution based on the Clarke–Wright Savings heuristic is able to minimise fleet distance per day by 75.5% (32.41 km out of 132.44 km). The First-Order Decay based estimates of the IPCC are Tier-1, ranging from 25,959 tonnes CO₂e per year monetised at ₹5.19 crores/year (927 tonnes CH₄/year). Ward savings over a year amount to an estimated ₹5.79 crores. The framework illustrates the combined use of satellite remote sensing, application of combinatorial optimization and environmental accounting as a single decision support architecture for civic managing units.

Despite being a tourism hub, Udupi is devastated by urban flooding, especially during the monsoons, when decades of accumulated waste can clog its sewer network and contaminate groundwater.

## 1. Introduction

Despite the estimated huge amount of MSW generated by urban municipalities across India (more than 150,000 tonnes per day), only a small fraction of cities utilise modern tools for Collection Plan such as paper maps, complaint registers, and institutional memory (CPCB, 2022). The Udupi CMC budget on solid waste operations is in the hundreds of crores per year, with challenges yet to be ironed out, such as routes that were created 20 years ago, no real-time detection system to identify the locations of new illegal dump sites and no system to estimate the level of methane at a single ward or in a city.

Not having spatial intelligence in administrative decision making is the root cause. As long as officials can't picture a gradient of waste, they can do nothing for financial implications and when policy decisions are made, waste management stays reactive.

This is where AstraCity comes in: AstraCity builds a virtual, continuously queryable Udupi CMC that mirrors the physical assets, models and optimizes operations, and estimates environmental impact. The twin is not a map dashboard, rather, it is a layered decision support framework in which each analytical module supports the next.

The four capabilities that are integrated into one pipeline are the core contribution:
1. The illegal dump risk screening (spatial surveillance layer) is derived from the Sentinel-2 LULC classification.
2. Waste modelling at the building level (flow layer) and the simulation of scenarios.
3. CVRPTW routing, both under road-width and time constraints, including optimization layer.
4. Estimate methane from IPCC models and economic scenarios (impact layer).

Udupi City (Udupi CMC) was chosen for the pilot area due to a combination of a high availability of data, coupled with structural features which are representative of the problems faced in dealing with a municipal solid waste catchment in Udupi. This was based on three criteria. First, the ward's urban morphology is the same as the one observed in many wards of Udupi CMC. Second, Udupi CMC has documented operational relevance. Third, the ward has open and reproducible data.

## 2. Digital Twin Architecture

### 2.1 Definition
AstraCity is a 'ward-level digital twin'—a virtual copy of Udupi CMC that is spatiotemporally grounded in both its physical waste management system and capabilities for monitoring and tracking wastes' trajectories for guiding decisions.

### 2.2 Physical Entities Mirrored

| Entity Layer | Components |
|---|---|
| Built Environment | 9,471 building footprints (residential, apartment, commercial, institutional) |
| Road Network | 2,027 classified segments (width, type, vehicle access) |
| Waste Infrastructure | 6 DWCCs, 1 BMU (Kudlu Gate, 2.10 km), Dumpyard (Yelahanka, 21.03 km) |
| Land Parcels | Open land polygons extracted from LULC classification |
| Administrative | Ward Boundary (Udupi CMC) |

### 2.3 State Variables
- **Static layers:** building footprints, road segments, DWCC locations, land-cover classes.
- **Dynamic layers:** Waste generation per grid cell, vehicle route assignments, risk score for dumps, methane estimates, economic estimates.

### 2.4 Analytical Modules and Data Flow
The twin is actually a 5-stage pipeline:
1. Sentinel-2 Imagery → MLC Classification
2. LULC Map → DBSCAN on Open Land pixels (Illegal Dump Risk Mapping)
3. Building-level occupancy model → Breakdown of waste generation grid (59 cells × 54 scenarios)
4. CVRPTW + Clarke–Wright → Distributed Systems (Routing & Assignment)
5. IPCC FOD + Economic model → Methane / CO₂e Estimates + Financial Impact

### 2.5 Twin Boundary
Unit 174 in the Ward is the unit that is being modeled. The external facilities represent linked terminal nodes which are reachable end nodes in the routing graph, but not modeled systems.

## 3. Related Work

### 3.1 Emission-Capacitated Vehicle Routing
For CVRP, typical formulations are those of minimizing distance or travel time. A further view employed is the concept of the "emission capacitated variant", where per-vehicle CO₂ emissions account for the total effect. Rabbani et al. [14] develop a green VRP for waste collection problem which minimizes a combination of distance and vehicle emissions.

### 3.2 Time-Dependent Travel Times
Trifa et al. [15] show that introducing time-of-day speed variation to CVRPTW changes the feasibility and overall emissions, especially with high congestion periods occurring during morning rush hours. 

### 3.3 IoT-Enabled Smart Bin Systems
Fill-level sensor networks on bins and DWCCs minimise unnecessary collection trips by sending dynamic updates of the routes whenever bins are over a fill threshold [16].

## 4. Methodology

### 4.1 Study Area and Data Sources
Udupi City is a new township in south-east of Udupi with 9,471 building footprints, an estimated population of ~1,65,401 and a daily waste load of 55 tonnes. All data were open access and datasets used included: Sentinel-2 Level-2A images, building footprints (OSM), road data (GeoFabrik), and Udupi CMC GIS shapefiles.

### 4.2 LULC Classification
The Sentinel-2 imagery were imported into QGIS 3.x and classified using the Semi-Automatic Classification Plugin (SCP) using Maximum Likelihood Classification (MLC). Four classes were identified: Built-up, Vegetation, Open Land, and Water Bodies. 

### 4.3 Illegal Dump Detection
To avoid dumping anomalies in open land polygons, a brightness threshold approach was used. The spatially adjacent candidate points were clustered into discrete dump sites using the DBSCAN.

### 4.4 Waste Generation Modelling (Flow Layer)
The ward was broken down into 59 active grid cells. Per-cell waste generation was determined as:
`Waste = (0.45 × P) + (2.8 × C) × multipliers`

**Key Waste Scenarios:**
| Scenario | Waste (Tonnes/day) | Change |
|---|---|---|
| Reference (Normal) | 14.08 | — |
| Heavy Rainfall | 16.19 | +15% |
| Ganesh Chaturthi | 18.02 | +28% |
| Compound Worst Case | 21.86 | +55% |

### 4.5 Route Optimization (CVRPTW Layer)
The collection problem is formulated as a Capacitated Vehicle Routing Problem with Time Windows (CVRPTW). 

**Fleet Structure:**
| Vehicle Type | Quantity | Capacity | Min Road Width |
|---|---|---|---|
| Auto-tipper | 12 | 500 kg | ≥ 2 m |
| Large compactor | 2 | 10,000 kg | ≥ 6 m |
| Small compactor | 2 | 5,000 kg | ≥ 4 m |
| Push cart | 8 | 200 kg | Any |
| Liquid tanker | 1 | 5,000 L | ≥ 9 m |

### 4.6 IoT Integration Layer (Design Specification)
A sensor integration layer is mandated as a future deployment objective and three data streams are defined: Fill level sensors at DWCCs, GPS logging at vehicles, and Weighbridge readings.

### 4.7 Methane Estimation and Economic Assessment
**Methane Estimation (IPCC Tier-1 FOD method):**
`CH₄ (tonnes/year) = MSW_organic × DOCf × MCF × (16/12) × (1 − OX)`

**Economic Model:**
| Benefit Stream | Annual Value | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Carbon credit revenue (₹2,000/t CO₂e) | ₹5.19 crores | 89.6% |
| Fuel savings (₹20/km × 100.03 km/day × 365) | ₹0.07 crores | 1.2% |
| Avoided dump cleanup (30 sites × ₹52,000) | ₹0.16 crores | 2.8% |
| Labour savings | ₹0.25 crores | 4.3% |
| Compost sales (digestate at ₹1,500/tonne) | ₹0.12 crores | 2.1% |
| **Total (Udupi CMC)** | **₹5.79 crores** | **100%** |

## 5. Results

### 5.1 LULC Classification
| Class | Area (km²) | Percentage | Description |
|---|---|---|---|
| Built-up | 11.85 | 64.1% | High-density residential/commercial fabric |
| Vegetation | 3.26 | 17.6% | Parks, avenue trees, private gardens |
| Open Land | 2.83 | 15.3% | Surveillance priority |
| Water Bodies | 0.56 | 3.0% | Agara Lake, Udupi City Lake |

### 5.2 Dump Risk Detection
Four potential illegal dump sites were found using DBSCAN. Two P0 sites present for same day intervention. 

### 5.3 Route Optimization
| Metric | Pre-Optimization | Post-Optimization | Improvement |
|---|---|---|---|
| Daily fleet distance | 132.44 km | 32.41 km | 75.5% reduction |
| Daily fuel cost | ₹2,072 | ₹507 | ₹1,565 saved/day |
| Network coverage | — | 95.3% | +15.3 pp |
| Average collection time | 4.5 hours/vehicle | 3 hours/vehicle | 33% reduction |

### 5.4 Environmental Impact
- **CH₄ emissions:** 927 tonnes/year
- **CO₂e:** 25,959 tonnes/year
- **Carbon credit value:** ₹5.19 crores/year
- **Biogas Energy Potential:** ~19 million kWh/year

## 6. Discussion

This publication integrates remote sensing, spatial risk screening, routing optimization, and impact analysis into a single decision support pipeline. Road width constraints were identified as critical, making a multi-tiered fleet essential for Udupi CMC.

The twin enables proactive capacity planning by simulating scenarios where waste can increase by up to 55%. Future integration with shop-floor IoT sensors will transition the system to near real-time tracking, reducing reliance on default averages.

---

## 7. Conclusion

AstraCity presents a proof-of-concept of a municipal solid waste management digital twin that can be created with no proprietary software or real-time sensor networks. The twin results in a 75.5% reduction in daily fleet distance, identifies hazardous dumping areas, tracks annual CO₂e emissions, and estimates annual savings of ₹5.79 crore.
